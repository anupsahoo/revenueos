# The decision I would never hand to an agent

**Draft.** The accept. The agent can read the brief, find the three closest
things we have built before, explain why each one fits, and write a POC plan
good enough to send. I let it do all of that, because it is faster than me and
it does not get bored on the fifteenth brief. What it does not do is press
Accept. Pressing Accept is not a document being finished; it is a promise to a
customer about what we will prove, in how many weeks, with which of their
systems, under their regulator. The person who makes that promise has to be the
person who will be in the room when it slips, and who can weigh the things that
are nowhere in the event log: that this bank's security review always takes
three weeks longer than they say, that this account is one bad POC away from
churning, that we do not really have the Guidewire people yet. The agent
optimises for the best match against what we have done before. A Solution
Architect decides what we are willing to be held to. So the loop is deliberately
asymmetric: everything up to the commitment is automated, and the commitment
itself stays with a named human, recorded as an event with their name on it.
